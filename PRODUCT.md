# Product

## Register

product

## Users
A broad, mixed audience on Windows 10/11: students, writers, office workers and power users who want to type with their voice in any app. They spend almost all of their time in other applications, holding Ctrl + Win to dictate; the floating bar is the surface they see most. The hub is opened briefly to check history, add dictionary words or snippets, adjust style, and change settings.

## Product Purpose
SpeakIt is a free, open-source, system-wide voice typing app that cleans up speech (fillers, self-corrections, Hinglish) and pastes formatted text at the cursor, online via Groq/Gemini or fully offline. Success is when the user forgets the tool exists: dictation is fast, the bar is calm and legible, and the hub answers any question in a glance.

## Brand Personality
Confident, quiet, energetic. Charcoal surfaces with a single vivid volt-green that means "live" or "go". Closest reference: the Spotify desktop app (dark charcoal, one green reserved for primary actions and playback state). Voice is plain and friendly, never salesy.

## Anti-references
- Emoji used as interface icons.
- Generic AI SaaS: cream/sand backgrounds, gradient text, glass cards, icon + heading + text card grids, uppercase eyebrows on every section.
- Neon overload: green used as decoration, glows everywhere, "gamer" RGB looks.

## Design Principles
1. The bar is the product. Every state of the floating bar must be readable in under a second, over any app.
2. Green means live. The accent marks recording, the primary action and the current selection, nothing else.
3. Earned familiarity. Standard desktop patterns (rail nav, lists, toggles, dialogs) done precisely beat invented affordances.
4. Approachable by default. Plain language, generous hit targets, sensible defaults; advanced settings stay out of the way.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Target WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 body text contrast in both themes, visible focus rings for full keyboard use, state never conveyed by color alone (icons + labels in the bar), and `prefers-reduced-motion` honored for the bar pulse and meter.
